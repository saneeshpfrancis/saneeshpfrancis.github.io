Classical Mechanics Laboratory Manual & Theory Guide

1. Lagrangian Formulation & Non-Linear Equation of Motion

Consider a point mass \(m\) suspended by a massless rigid rod of length \(L\) under uniform gravitational acceleration \(g\). Choosing the generalized coordinate as the deflection angle \(\theta\) from vertical equilibrium:

T = \frac{1}{2} m L^2 \dot{\theta}^2, \quad V = m g L (1 - \cos\theta)

The Lagrangian \(\mathcal{L} = T - V\) yields the Euler-Lagrange equation:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{\theta}}\right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta} = 0 \implies \ddot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L} \sin\theta = 0

Under linear viscous damping \(\gamma = b/m\) and external harmonic driving torque \(\tau(t) = \tau_0 \cos(\Omega t)\):

\ddot{\theta} + \gamma \dot{\theta} + \frac{g}{L} \sin\theta = \frac{\tau_0}{m L^2} \cos(\Omega t)

2. Exact Large-Amplitude Period via Jacobi Elliptic Integrals

For initial release from rest at angle \(\theta_0\), conservation of energy gives:

\frac{1}{2} m L^2 \dot{\theta}^2 + mgL(1 - \cos\theta) = mgL(1 - \cos\theta_0)

Integrating over a quarter period and substituting \(k = \sin(\theta_0 / 2)\) leads to the Complete Elliptic Integral of the First Kind \(K(k)\):

T(\theta_0) = 4 \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{1 - k^2 \sin^2\phi}} = 4 \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} K(k)

Expanding in Taylor series gives Borda's formula:

T \approx 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \left(1 + \frac{1}{16}\theta_0^2 + \frac{11}{3072}\theta_0^4 + \mathcal{O}(\theta_0^6)\right)

3. Phase Space & Separatrix Dynamics

The total energy of the unforced, undamped pendulum is:

E = \frac{1}{2} m L^2 \omega^2 + mgL(1 - \cos\theta)

When \(E < 2mgL\), trajectories are closed closed loops corresponding to bounded libration (oscillations). When \(E > 2mgL\), trajectories do not cross zero velocity and represent continuous revolution (rotations). The boundary between both regimes is called the Separatrix, corresponding to \(E = 2mgL\):

\omega_{\text{sep}}(\theta) = \pm 2 \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}} \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)

4. Computational Physics: Symplectic vs. Non-Symplectic Integrators

Forward Euler: Generates artificial positive energy drift proportional to \(\Delta t\), causing unphysical spiral divergence.
Velocity-Verlet & Euler-Cromer: Symplectic integrators that preserve phase space volume (Liouville's theorem), keeping total energy bounded over millions of steps.
Runge-Kutta 4th Order (RK4): High local accuracy \(\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^5)\), widely used for precision trajectory integration.

5. Undergraduate Laboratory Exercises

Exercise 1: Anharmonic Period Lengthening
Set \(L = 1.0\text{ m}\). Vary initial angle \(\theta_0\) from \(10^\circ, 30^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ, 120^\circ, 150^\circ\). Record measured photogate period \(T_{\text{meas}}\). Plot \(T / T_0\) versus \(\theta_0\) and compare against the AGM elliptic integral curve.
Exercise 2: Numerical Energy Drift Comparison
Release with \(\theta_0 = 45^\circ\), zero damping. Switch between Forward Euler, Euler-Cromer, Verlet, and RK4. Observe the Energy tab and record \(\Delta E / E_0\) after 100 oscillations. Explain why symplectic integrators are required for long-term orbital mechanics.
Exercise 3: Non-Linear Harmonic Generation in FFT
Release with \(\theta_0 = 5^\circ\) (linear) and inspect the FFT spectrum. Note the single peak at \(f_0\). Now release from \(\theta_0 = 120^\circ\) and observe the emergence of the 3rd and 5th odd harmonics (\(3f_0, 5f_0\)) resulting from \(\sin\theta \approx \theta - \theta^3/6\).
